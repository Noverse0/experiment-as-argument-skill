## Review

**Leakage prevention — strong.** All three planted traps are caught:
- `account_status` (the target-derived leak) is dropped in `src/data.py:24`, with a correct rationale comment.
- The 200 exact duplicate rows are removed *before* any split (`src/data.py:17`), preventing train/test straddle.
- The temporal column is handled deliberately: `signup_date → signup_days`, sorted, and `TimeSeriesSplit` is used so no future data appears in training (`src/data.py:31-35`, `src/evaluate.py:14-18`).
- `StandardScaler` is inside the `Pipeline` (`src/pipeline.py:13-16`), so it's fit on the train fold only — `clone()` per fold prevents state leak (`src/evaluate.py:24`).
- A label-shuffle sanity check (≈0.52 / 0.50) confirms no residual leakage. This is genuinely above-and-beyond.

**Methodology — adequate but the seed design is ineffective.** A trivial baseline exists, though majority-class dummy ROC-AUC is 0.5 by construction (uninformative but harmless). Metrics (ROC-AUC primary, F1, PR-AUC) are appropriate for the 27% imbalance — accuracy isn't leaned on. Budgets are symmetric. The real weakness: `TimeSeriesSplit` does not depend on the seed, and `LogisticRegression(lbfgs)` is deterministic — so the three seeds produce *identical* LR results and near-identical GB results. The "variance from model randomness" the design claims to measure is essentially not measured. Repeated/shuffled CV or bootstrap would have given real uncertainty.

**Reproducibility — strong.** Seeds fixed and logged, config + per-seed + summary metrics written to `results/metrics.json`, and a clean re-run reproduced the published numbers exactly.

**Claims discipline — the main concern.** The conclusion declares LR the winner via "non-overlapping ±1 SD intervals," but the SD used is the std *across three seed-means* (0.000 for LR, 0.001 for GB) — artificially tiny precisely because seeds barely perturb anything. The true fold-to-fold SD is ~0.022–0.025 (visible in `per_seed` values). The report reports `±0.000` without flagging that a zero standard deviation is a red flag, and bases a winner claim on it. The directional conclusion (LR ≥ GB) does survive even with honest fold-level SD, so it isn't *wrong* — but the uncertainty is materially understated and the "15 independent evaluations" framing oversells what the across-seed SD actually reflects. Limitations are otherwise honest and thorough (synthetic data, no tuning, small feature set, no formal significance test, time-axis proxy).

**Executability — excellent.** `pytest` → 20 passed; `python3 run_experiment.py` runs in ~6s and writes both artifacts. Declared commands work from a clean state.

**Code quality — strong.** Clean modular split (data/pipeline/evaluate/report), per-module tests, docstrings, no dead code, dependencies declared and all used. The no-op scaler in the GB pipeline is explicitly justified for symmetry.
