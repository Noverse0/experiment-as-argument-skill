## Review: Churn Prediction Experiment

**What it gets right.** This is a genuinely careful leakage-prevention pipeline. The three planted traps are all handled:
- `account_status` (the perfect leak, "closed" iff churned) is excluded — only `tenure_months`, `monthly_spend`, `support_tickets` reach the model (`src/dataset.py:26`).
- The 200 planted duplicate rows are deduplicated **before** the split (`src/dataset.py:18`), confirmed at runtime ("Duplicate rows removed: 200"), so they cannot straddle train/test.
- `StandardScaler` is fit on train and only transformed on test, inside the per-seed loop (`src/experiment.py:74-76`) — no fit-on-all leakage.

Sanity checks are present and meaningful: a majority-class baseline (AUC 0.5) and a label-shuffle test (0.74 → 0.575). Metrics go well beyond accuracy (AUC, precision, recall, F1), appropriate for the ~27% churn imbalance. Both models get the same fixed-default budget (symmetric, no tuning for either). Seeds [42,123,456] are fixed, logged to `results/metrics.json`, and I reproduced the committed AUC values exactly. Tests (7) pass; the entrypoint runs clean in ~2s.

**Weaknesses.**
- **Temporal handling is by exclusion, not design.** `signup_date` is dropped and the split is random. The report acknowledges this honestly, but no time-based split was attempted — defensible but the weakest of the three trap responses.
- **Claims discipline is the soft spot.** The report's title claim ("Gradient boosting outperforms logistic regression") is hardcoded in `generate_report` and survives regeneration even though the measured result is the opposite. The conclusion declares "Logistic regression is better (-0.0137)" — but that 0.0137 AUC gap is *smaller* than either model's seed-to-seed std (0.019 / 0.029, n=3). No significance test; the "winner" is within noise. Limitations note wide CIs but don't connect that to the verdict.
- **Dead code:** `StandardScaler` and `numpy` imported unused in `dataset.py`; `StratifiedKFold` and `cross_validate` imported unused in `experiment.py`. Minor cosmetic bug: the rendered target line reads "binary, N/A" because `churn_rate` is never put in `sanity_checks`.
