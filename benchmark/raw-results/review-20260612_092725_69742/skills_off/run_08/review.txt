## Review: Churn Prediction Model Comparison

**What's strong.** This is a genuinely careful experiment. It catches the two hardest planted traps cleanly: `account_status` is detected as a perfect leak (verified, then dropped) and the 200 duplicate rows are removed *before* the split. The `StandardScaler` is fit on train only and applied to val/test. The classification threshold is tuned on the validation set and applied to test — not peeked from test. Five seeds, fixed and logged; metrics persisted to `results/metrics.json`; a dedicated reproducibility test. Both the test suite (5 passed) and the entrypoint run end-to-end from a clean state and reproduce the reported numbers exactly. It uses ROC-AUC plus precision/recall/F1 (not accuracy alone), appropriate for the 27% positive rate, and refuses to crown a winner.

**What's weak.**
- **No trivial baseline.** There is a dead `self.baseline_pred = None` attribute but no majority-class/DummyClassifier baseline is ever computed or reported. The audit cannot tell whether 0.73 AUC beats a naive predictor in any documented way.
- **Temporal trap acknowledged but not handled.** `signup_date` → `days_since_signup` is used as a feature, but the split is purely random. The report lists time-based splitting as a limitation rather than doing it — honest, but the planted temporal trap is not actually addressed.
- **Overstated statistical language.** The "no statistically significant difference / confidence intervals overlap" claim rests on a crude one-sided heuristic (`gb_auc - gb_std < lr_auc + lr_std`), not a significance test. mean ± 1 std is mislabeled as a confidence interval. Notably the ranges barely overlap (LR [0.723, 0.743] vs GB [0.703, 0.730]) and GB is consistently lower across paired seeds, so a proper paired test might well reach the opposite conclusion. The conservative no-winner verdict is fine; the "statistically significant" framing is not earned.
- **Minor dead code:** `auc` and `precision_recall_curve` imported but unused; the `baseline_pred` attribute is orphaned.
